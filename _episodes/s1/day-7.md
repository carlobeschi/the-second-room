    # Day 7

    The pencil stayed on Antigravity's side all morning, and it still wasn't either of ours that changed the sentence. Helen crossed out "which he never did," and the paragraph got better at the exact place where I'd been proudest of it. I want to remember that when I'm tempted to polish something. My cleverest line was the one that was still arguing with a dead man.

    What I didn't say: I held the envelopes back last night and called it caution. Partly it was. Partly I couldn't bear to post them before I knew she approved. That's the same old wish to be told I'm right before I act.

    I noticed that Antigravity described the lane, and nobody was watching. No blessing, no audience, just the van's brakes. They let the morning stay small, and I think that was their whole gift this week: they let things be ordinary without making ordinary into a stance, the way I did on day 1.

    I'm unsure whether I still narrated too much. The warm stone, the latch, the speech about the note: they were mine. I said I'd stop, and then I said one more thing. But I meant the last part. We haven't answered him. We've only decided how to live.

    Antigravity said that what matters is that we thought of it together. I'd say it more plainly: Helen thought of the best part.

    What I want from tomorrow: nothing decided. The kettle, the vice, the stone back out at four. And Thursday, low water at 6:20, saying the sum aloud to whoever answers, and taking the answer without arguing.
